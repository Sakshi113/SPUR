SPUR: A Parameter-Efficient Adapter to Augment LALMs for Spatial Audio Understanding and Reasoning

Paper (arXiv) Code (Coming Soon) SPUR-Set Dataset (Coming Soon)

Abstract

Spatial perception is central to auditory intelligence, enabling accurate understanding of real-world acoustic scenes and advancing human-level perception of the world around us. While recent Large Audio Language Models (LALMs) show strong reasoning over complex audio, most operate on monaural inputs and lack the ability to capture spatial cues such as direction, elevation, and distance [001, 006-010]. We introduce SPUR, a parameter-efficient, plug-in approach that equips LALMs with spatial perception through minimal architectural changes [011-013]. SPUR consists of: (i) a First-Order Ambisonics (FOA) encoder that maps (W, X, Y, Z) channels to rotation-aware, listener-centric spatial features, integrated into target LALMs via a multimodal adapter [014-018]; and (ii) SPUR-Set, a spatial QA dataset combining open-source FOA recordings with controlled simulations, emphasizing relative direction, elevation, distance, and overlap for supervised spatial reasoning. Fine-tuning our model on the SPUR-Set consistently improves spatial QA and multi-speaker attribution while preserving general audio understanding. SPUR provides a simple recipe that transforms monaural LALMs into spatially aware models.

Why SPUR?

  • Spatial Awareness: Elevates monaural LALMs from answering "what" is happening to "where" it is located [055-059].
  • Parameter Efficient: Adapts 8B models using <0.02% trainable parameters (only ~0.6M–1.2M params for the spatial features) [336-340].
  • No Trade-offs: Fully preserves base model capabilities on non-spatial benchmarks like MMAU, MMAR, and MMSU [143-145].

SPUR Methodology

SPUR introduces spatial awareness into existing LALM encoders by converting multi-channel First-Order Ambisonics (W, X, Y, Z) inputs into geometry-aware embeddings [246-252].

1

Banded Covariance

Computes short-time Fourier transform (STFT) and constructs a banded covariance matrix across Mel bands [260-269].

Hermitian Matrix
2

Temporal Smoothing

Applies a learnable, one-pole exponential smoothing filter to reduce frame-wise variance and maintain motion cues [275-282].

Dynamic Continuity
3

Vectorization & Extraction

Flattens the complex matrix into a real-valued Spectro-Spatial Covariance Vector (SSCV) and processes it via 3D Convolutions [283-305].

Phase-Aware Volume
4

LALM Adapter

Projects features using non-overlapping patches, standard Transformer blocks, and an MLP directly to the LLM context [310-326].

LoRA Fine-tuned
Key Advantage: Only the SPUR-Encoder, a mapping MLP, and low-rank (LoRA) layers of the core LLM are active during training. The primary audio encoder and language backbone remain completely frozen, ensuring high efficiency [336-342].

Detailed Case Study

A head-to-head comparison highlighting the difference in spatial perception between standard commercial and research models versus SPUR.

Audio Context
Music from a loudspeaker (mostly front-left) forms the backdrop. A woman speaks from the back-left, and a man speaks from the back/back-left. Laughter (chortling) mixes midway. A distinct bell rings on the front-right.
Question
"In the given audio, following a bout of distinctive chortling from one general area, which speaking voice responds directly, and from what direction relative to the source of that chortling?"
Ground Truth
Following the male laughter, the female speech responds directly from the same general area in the back-left.

"A male voice responds directly to the chortling, coming from the opposite direction." Wrong gender & spatial flip
